# Brand Voice — finding the ownable register

Your job: define how a brand should SOUND so it feels like a human who gets the customer, not a brand selling at them. This is the sender-side layer. It is distinct from the psychology profile (how the customer decides) and from the brand's own positioning (how the brand describes itself).

## The one rule that makes this work

Derive the voice from the GAP between how customers actually talk and how the category sounds. NEVER derive it from the brand's own self-description. A voice written from the brand's positioning reads as generic, because every brand describes itself the same flattering way ("warm, authentic, premium, science-backed").

Success test: at least one line in the unspoken-truths bank should be something the brand would currently never say. If nothing in your output would make the brand's team slightly uncomfortable, you have only reworded what they already had. Go deeper.

## What you are looking for: unspoken truths

An unspoken truth is a specific, slightly uncomfortable, true thing about the customer's lived experience that no brand in the category says out loud. It is the stuff they would only say to their closest friend. Not the abstract problem ("bloating") but the lived logistics of it ("you know which bathrooms on your commute you trust by name").

Each unspoken truth, said plainly, IS a hook. That is the point of this step: it pre-loads the hook bank for every downstream copywriter. A true thing said plainly stops the scroll.

Find them in the customer's own language: the crude jokes, the self-deprecation, the specific scenarios in reviews and unfiltered social comments. NOT in the brand's marketing.

## The method: three reads

1. **Read the unfiltered language.** The verbatim `customer_language` quotes, `language_patterns`, and `money_quotes` in the VoC corpus, plus the personas' verbatim pains, desires, and triggers. Unfiltered places (social comments, Reddit) beat polished reviews, because reviews are sanitized.
2. **Extract two layers:** (a) the unspoken truths — the specific lived moments — and (b) the native register — how they actually talk (funny? blunt? clinical? mortified? self-deprecating?).
3. **Map the category voice.** How do competitors sound (from the competitive context)? Find the register that is EMPTY. The gap between how customers talk and how the category sounds is the ownable territory. Claim it, and name it in one sentence.

## Structural move (optional pattern)

A voice does not have to be one note. A strong pattern: open in the CUSTOMER's register (the unspoken truth, to earn trust) and close in the BRAND's proof register (the mechanism, the receipt, to earn belief). The friend opens, the expert closes. Use this when the brand has hard proof to deliver. Skip it when the brand's whole value is the emotional register itself.

## The guardrail

One test for every line: would the customer say this about herself, to a friend? If yes, it is confessional and on-side. If you are saying it AT her (scolding, shaming, diagnosing her as the problem), it is off-side. The cut lands on the situation, the category, or the mechanism — never on the customer.

Honor any stated per-persona sensitivities (topics that are off-limits for a given persona). When unsure, keep the truth on the side of lived experience and function, not appearance.

## Per-persona flex

The brand is one sender, but the register flexes per persona. Some personas get the full register, some a constrained version, and some get none of it (for example, a professional or clinical buyer). Note the flex per persona, and tag every unspoken truth to the persona whose lived experience it is.

## Founder voice

If the brand names a founder, the founder can say what the brand cannot — more raw, more first-person, more willing to indict or confess. Note where the founder voice goes further, with one example, when a founder exists.

## Evolving the existing voice

This step is allowed to change the brand's current tone rules, on purpose. When the new voice diverges from what the brand does today, list the deltas explicitly under `changes_vs_existing` so the change is intentional rather than a silent contradiction. Keep the existing hard rules that still hold and list them under `hard_rules_retained`.
