Sample Content Ideas

This document shows you what to expect when you ask Claude for content ideas using the secondkind-bold-organic skill.

How to read each example:

  1. We start with a real customer complaint pulled from the matrix
  2. You see the exact prompt you’d type into Claude
  3. You see the three content ideas Claude would suggest back

The three ideas are intentionally different. Claude gives you variety so you can pick the right one for your week, a quick scroll-stopper if you’re short on time, a heavier emotional piece if you’ve got more bandwidth, a brand-positioning post if it’s a Monday and you need to anchor the week, etc.

After the 10 examples, the final section shows you what happens when you pick one of the ideas and ask Claude for the full details (“go deep on #2”).

Example 1: “60 days on a probiotic, nothing changed. Cancelled my order.”

Matrix entry: pain-001

You type:

Use secondkind-bold-organic. Give me 3 variations for pain-001.

Claude gives you 3 ideas:

Idea 1: A bold statement post (Instagram)

A clean square Instagram post with the headline:

“We’re not probiotics. We don’t want to be.”

Below it, three short proof lines: - Most probiotics don’t survive your stomach acid. - The ones that do don’t colonize. - We send the compounds your gut actually uses, already active.

What it looks like: A casual phone-snap photo of a kitchen counter at morning, with the text laid over the empty upper part of the photo. SK Bold wordmark in the corner.

Why it works: Customers who’ve already cancelled don’t want another sales pitch. They want validation that giving up was rational. This post says “the whole category was the problem” in four lines.

Idea 2: A long letter video (TikTok)

A short video where text appears line-by-line over silent footage of a kitchen at evening. Reads like a personal letter to the customer who just quit.

Opens with:

Listen, to the women who cancelled their probiotic at month two.

It then walks through why probiotics plateau (the bacteria die in stomach acid, the survivors can’t colonize an established gut) before introducing postbiotics as the alternative.

What it looks like: Silent B-roll, a hand opening a probiotic bottle, a half-eaten dinner plate, a kitchen at dusk. No talking, just the text telling the story.

Why it works: When customers feel betrayed by a product, a long validating letter does what a short post can’t, explains the WHY behind their disappointment.

Idea 3: A 60-second AI talking-head video

The recurring SecondKind Bold AI avatar (the same woman who appears in every AI video) speaks directly to camera across 5 quick scenes:

  1. “I cancelled my probiotic at day 47. Three years of paying $50 a month for nothing.”
  2. “Clean eating, walks at lunch, I did everything. Nothing changed.”
  3. “Turns out 70% of probiotic bacteria die in stomach acid. The few survivors can’t colonize. It wasn’t me. It was the model.”
  4. “So I switched to postbiotics. The compounds bacteria make, already active.”
  5. “Two weeks in. I forgot I was even taking anything.”

What it looks like: An AI-generated woman in a kitchen. Same face, same kitchen, same lighting across every video we make using this format.

Why it works: A first-person confession lands harder than a static post. And because the avatar is the same woman across many videos, viewers start recognizing her, that’s the brand recall engine.

Going Deep, what happens when you pick one idea

After Claude gives you the 3 ideas above, you pick one and ask for the full details. Here’s what that looks like.

Continuing from Example 1. You picked Idea 1 (the bold “We’re not probiotics” post) and you type:

Go deep on #1. I need full copy and visuals.

Claude gives you the full production package:

THE POST

Headline (large, brand serif font):

We’re not probiotics. We don’t want to be.

Body (smaller, brand sans-serif, 3 lines):

Most probiotics don’t survive your stomach acid. The ones that do don’t colonize. We send the compounds your gut actually uses, already active.

Bottom-right corner: SK Bold wordmark. No “shop now,” no link, no hashtags.

THE VISUAL

A phone-snap photo of a kitchen counter at morning. The text sits in the upper-left clear area of the photo.

The exact instruction to paste into Higgsfield:

"Shot on iPhone, candid. A clean wooden kitchen counter at morning, soft natural
light from a window off-frame, mostly empty counter with a single ceramic mug in
the bottom-right corner, large clear/negative space in the upper portion of the
frame, lived-in domestic feel, 1:1 aspect ratio, slightly imperfect framing. No
text, no captions, no overlays, no graphics, no typography."

Claude will also give you alternative backgrounds (bathroom counter, nightstand, open journal on desk) so you can rotate the visual across posts.

THE STEPS

  1. Open Claude. Get the copy + Higgsfield prompt above.
  2. Paste the Higgsfield prompt into Higgsfield. Generate the photo. Download it.
  3. Open Canva. Use the brand template. Drop the photo in.
  4. Add the headline (Caslon Condensed) and body (Neue Montreal) in the clear upper area of the photo.
  5. Add a small yellow highlight on the word compounds.
  6. Place SK Bold wordmark bottom-right.
  7. Export, post to Instagram.

CHECKS CLAUDE WILL HAVE ALREADY DONE

  • Voice sounds right (confrontational, science-armed, not wellness-influencer)
  • No prohibited words (no miracle, journey, holistic, transform, etc.)
  • No “shop now” CTA
  • Doesn’t name a competitor, attacks the category, not Seed or Ritual
  • Background photo has a clear area for the text

How to use these examples

For practice: Read through a few and try to predict what Claude would suggest before reading the answer. You’ll quickly get a feel for which kinds of content suit which kinds of customer complaints.

For your own week: When you sit down to make content, open matrix.md, pick a real customer complaint, ask Claude for variations, and pick one to go deep on. The flow is exactly what these examples show.

If you ever want more examples: Pick any matrix entry and ask Claude for variations. The skill works the same way for every entry, you’ll get 3 ideas back, you pick one, you ask for the full details.
