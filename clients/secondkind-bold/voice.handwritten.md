# SecondKind Bold — Voice

The sender-side layer: who the brand is and how it sounds, as distinct from who the customer is. This extends the existing bold-voice rules in this folder. Where the two conflict, this is the newer thinking and wins (most importantly: we no longer always lead with the mechanism, see "What this changes" at the end). Scope: Phase 1 paid acquisition, landing pages, and founder content.

## The territory we own

The bathroom-and-bedroom logistics of a bad gut, said in the language you'd only use with your closest friend. The specific, slightly mortifying, true stuff that's "too much" for a premium wellness brand.

It's open because nobody in the category will touch it. The whole space talks about "bloating" as a tasteful abstract noun. We say the part she only texts her group chat. (Internal note: this is competitive whitespace. Never name a competitor in customer-facing copy.)

## How we sound: the friend opens, the scientist closes

Same four-beat arc as before, but the register switches halfway. The friend earns the trust, then the scientist gives her the reason to believe.

- Beat 1, the suspicion she already had: confessional. Her exact day, in her words. Not the stat.
- Beat 2, the mechanism: clinical. The receipt, unchanged (the 70% transit death, the trials).
- Beat 3, the vindication: confessional. "You weren't wrong" lands hard because beat 1 proved we know her.
- Beat 4, the offer: calm, clinical. The guarantee closes, it never opens.

The brand stops being only a lawyer and becomes a friend who opens the conversation and a scientist who closes it. Every receipt stays. The human gets added.

## The line we won't cross

One test: would she say this about herself, to a friend? "You unbuttoned your jeans in the car" passes (rueful, true, hers). "Stop letting your gut run your life" fails (we're scolding her). Stay on her side of that line and the existing rule holds: the cut lands on the category or the mechanism, never the customer.

Everything else from the existing rules still stands: no competitor names, no scarcity or urgency, FDA structure-function language in fine print, sentence case, F37 Caslon and Neue Montreal only.

## How the register flexes by persona

- **Danielle**: full confessional. Bathroom and body. She owns this language.
- **Paula**: confessional, body permitted, lead on the medical dismissal ("told you you're fine, you know you're not").
- **Isaac**: confessional but performance-coded. Logistics and reliability, never the body.
- **Natalie**: confessional is a gift here, but emotional and identity only. Never her body, never her weight. Sacred ground.
- **Brandon**: confessional in a dry, analytical key. The quiet disillusionment of the optimizer whose one variable never moved.
- **Paul**: no confessional. He stays clinical, peer to peer. His unspoken truth is professional, not bodily.

## The unspoken-truths bank

The raw material for hooks. Each line is already a hook, because a true thing said plainly stops the scroll. Pull from here before inventing anything new.

**Danielle**
- You know which bathrooms on your commute you trust. By name.
- You've left a dinner early and blamed an early morning.
- You eat the same six safe foods because everything else is a gamble.
- You've skipped the outfit you actually wanted to wear.
- You unbuttoned your jeans in the car before you even got home.

**Paula**
- You ate a salad. By 7pm you looked four months pregnant.
- Your doctor ran the labs and said everything's fine. You left knowing it wasn't.
- You dread dinners out. Not the food. What your body does after.
- You used to be regular. Now you have no idea what your body will do on any given day.

**Isaac**
- You get sick at the worst possible time. Third year running.
- You've built your calendar around an immune system you can't count on and called it bad luck.
- You hit a wall at 2pm you can't think your way through.
- You want things that actually do something, not another box to check.

**Natalie**
- Nobody warned you about the part where you don't feel like yourself.
- The fog you can't blame on the baby anymore.
- Everyone said it gets better. No one said you'd feel like a stranger to your own life.
- You're exhausted, but you know it's more than the sleep.

**Brandon**
- You optimized everything. The one thing that never moved was your gut.
- Twelve months of a premium probiotic. Nothing you track ever changed.
- At some point you just decided this is how your gut works.
- You don't want another product. You want to know why none of them worked.

**Paul** (clinical, not confessional)
- Your patients came back and told you the probiotic did nothing. The category never gave you the explanation.

## Two worked examples

**Danielle, problem aware:**
> You know which bathrooms on your commute you trust. You've left dinners early and blamed an early morning. You eat the same handful of safe foods because everything else is a gamble.
>
> Here's what nobody told you. Roughly 70% of the probiotic you kept buying died before it reached your gut. The ones that survived had to colonize, and most never do.
>
> You weren't doing it wrong. You were doing everything right with a product that never showed up.
>
> Postbiotics are the compounds those bacteria were supposed to make. We deliver them straight, already active. Try Gut Balance for 60 days. If your gut doesn't feel different, you don't pay.

**Natalie, problem aware** (the hardest constraint, body off-limits):
> Nobody warned you about this part. Not the weight. The part where you don't quite recognize yourself. The fog you can't blame on the baby anymore.
>
> Here's what no one mentioned: pregnancy restructures your gut, and your gut runs more of your energy and clarity than anyone told you. The probiotic you tried couldn't help, because most of it dies before it reaches the part that matters.
>
> You're not imagining it, and you're not failing at recovery. The tool was wrong, not you.
>
> Postbiotics deliver what your gut needs directly, no live bacteria required. Clean, third-party tested, no refrigeration. Try Gut Balance for 60 days. If you don't feel more like yourself, you don't pay.

## Founder voice (Remy)

The founder can say what the brand can't, including the genuinely raw. The brand diagnoses. Remy indicts and confesses.

> "My wife had a mental map of every bathroom between our apartment and her office. She didn't tell me for a year, because it's not exactly first-date conversation. That's what living with this actually looks like. And nobody in this industry sells to her. They sell to a stock photo of a woman doing yoga on a beach."

## What this changes vs the current rules

- **Old:** lead with the mechanism. **New:** lead with the confessional truth. The mechanism is beat 2, not the opener.
- **Old:** one register, prosecutorial and clinical throughout. **New:** confessional opens, clinical closes.
- **Unchanged:** body language only when the blame lands on the mechanism. Natalie's body stays sacred ground.
- **Unchanged:** no competitor names, no scarcity, FDA compliance, sentence case, brand typefaces.
