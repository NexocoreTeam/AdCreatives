## Brand Overview

Saved by Grace Co. (SBG Co.) is a Charlotte, NC-based faith-forward lifestyle apparel brand making graphic tees, sweatshirts, hats, and home decor for modern American moms and their kids. Operating as a 7-figure D2C business with 4,000+ boutique wholesale accounts on Faire, SBG Co. has spent 10 years carving out a distinct lane: faith that doesn't feel churchy, mom humor that doesn't apologize, and America pride that's more "summer cookout" than political statement. Featured on the Today Show. Made to order in-house. Every piece is a walking declaration of something she actually believes.

---

## Brand Story & Origin

SBG Co. was founded in Charlotte, NC approximately 10 years ago (est. ~2015). The brand grew from what appears to be a founder-led small shop — the homepage copy reveals a first-person voice ("Never in a million years did I believe we would expand the way we have. I am very grateful for each and every one of you willing to wait.") that points to a single founder who built the brand from the ground up without employees in the early days.

The origin story is rooted in the belief that faith-inspired apparel didn't have to feel dated, preachy, or unstylish — that a woman could wear her belief, her humor, and her love for her kids and her country on a single tee and look good doing it. The brand name itself — Saved by Grace — is the theological center, but the execution is always lifestyle-first.

The brand operates on a made-to-order model, framed as a sustainability choice ("Not carrying stock allows us to only provide what is needed, which in return helps diminish fast fashion and promotes eco-responsibility") and a quality guarantee ("Each item is made to order, ensuring quality").

Today, SBG Co. is a scaled operation: 7-figure D2C revenue, 4,600+ Faire orders, 4,000+ boutique wholesale partners, national TV exposure (Today Show), and a catalog of 783+ products across women's apparel, kids/baby, home decor, hats, accessories, and giftables.

## What Makes Them Different

SBG Co.'s positioning is built on a clear category indictment: faith apparel that feels too churchy, too dated, or too hard to actually style and wear. They solve this by being a **lifestyle brand first that happens to be faith-forward** — not a "Christian store" that happens to make tees.

**The five pillars of differentiation:**

1. **Tone** — Faith without preachiness. Humor without vulgarity. America pride without aggression. This is the hardest thing to get right and the most defensible moat.
2. **Made to order in Charlotte, NC** — Not dropshipped from overseas. Made in-house. This is a proof of craft, a local story, and an anti-fast-fashion message all in one.
3. **Mom-and-mini merchandising** — Women's and kids/baby designs are intentionally created to coordinate. This creates multi-unit purchase behavior and strong gifting utility.
4. **Message lanes that serve one buyer** — Faith, mom humor, patriotic, and lifestyle humor aren't separate audiences. They're different moods of the same woman on different days.
5. **10 years of community loyalty** — A built and returning customer base who "keeps coming back," verified buyers who rave in reviews, and wholesale scale (4,000+ boutiques) that validates real demand.

**In their own words (homepage copy):**
- "Wear What You Believe — Faith-filled pieces designed to share truth, spark hope, and point people back to Jesus."
- "Motherhood, Worn Honestly — Tees made for the joyful, exhausting, funny, and deeply meaningful parts of being a mom."
- "Made for Little Personalities — Playful, meaningful tees created for all the personality, wonder, and joy of childhood."
- "Personalized Craftmanship. Each item is made to order, ensuring quality. Made in North Carolina, USA."

## The Alternative Solution

Before SBG Co., this woman had two bad options:

**Option A:** Faith apparel from a Christian bookstore or mass-market brand — overly churchy, dated design sensibility, fonts from 2003, feels like a tract not a tee. Hard to style. Easy to mock. Won't wear it to Target.

**Option B:** Generic lifestyle or mom humor tees (Etsy, Amazon, boutique graphic tees) — either too secular and never quite her, or funny without meaning, with no faith dimension at all.

SBG Co. is the first option that doesn't make her choose between being stylish, being a mom, being a believer, and being an American. She doesn't have to compartmentalize. The tee does all four at once.

## Brand Voice & Tone

SBG Co.'s voice is the equivalent of a confident, funny, warm Christian mom who doesn't need to prove anything to anyone. She's not performing her faith. She's not apologizing for her humor. She's just herself — and she's stylish while she's at it.

**Adjectives:** Warm, direct, unpretentious, sincere, witty, grounded, community-first, a little proud (of her faith, her kids, her country)

**Patterns from site copy:**
- Headlines are punchy, declarative, often a little poetic: "Motherhood, Worn Honestly." "Wear What You Believe." "Made for Little Personalities."
- CTAs are lowercase-friendly and editorial: "SHOP NOW," "shop Best Sellers"
- Product names do the heavy lifting — they ARE the brand voice: "Mind Your Own Motherhood," "Put It On My Husband's Tab," "It's Not an Addiction, It's a Personality Trait," "Raising Em Feral," "Loves Jesus + America, Too"
- First-person founder voice shows up in operational copy: "Never in a million years did I believe we would expand the way we have. I am very grateful for each and every one of you willing to wait!"
- Review responses are warm and specific, not templated

**What they avoid:**
- Preachy, scripture-heavy, sermon-adjacent language
- Overly polished corporate tone
- Edgy, crude, or politically aggressive content
- Anything that would feel out of place in a modern boutique

**Visual voice (from Gemini analysis):** The brand's aesthetic is modern farmhouse meets faith-based mommy blog — warm, muted earth tones (oatmeal, washed-out denim, faded terracotta, warm greys), garment-dyed tees with short all-caps phrases, shiplap and pale wood in backgrounds, warm hazy afternoon light. Photography is a mix of high-quality UGC lifestyle shots and clean minimalist product flat lays. The overall mood is: wholesome, aspirational, comfortable, sincere, grounded. Visual references: Magnolia/Joanna Gaines, small-town boutique Instagram, clean text-based Christian apparel.
