# Strategy Matrix: Saved by Grace Co. (SBG Co.)

*3 persona(s) × 5 Schwartz awareness stages = 15 messaging cells*

## All-Of-It Amber *(primary)*

### Unaware

**Angle:** She is already dressing herself in fragments of her identity — this brand is what happens when someone finally builds the whole picture for her.

**What they know:** She buys graphic tees from boutiques and Target. She wears her faith, her mom identity, and her humor in separate layers — different shirts for different contexts. She has never consciously articulated that these pieces of her identity could or should live on the same shirt.

**Gap to fill:** There is a brand built exactly for the woman she already is — faith-forward, funny, and unflinchingly herself — where the kids pieces coordinate with hers so the whole family looks intentional without looking like a costume.

**Hook style:** pattern-interrupt / identity-mirror

**Example hook:** *"You are the same woman at church, at Target, and at the cookout. Why does your wardrobe act like you are three different people?"*

**Framework:** `pas` | **Mechanic:** Identity Mirror — open with a visual of three outfits side by side (church tee, generic boutique tee, mom humor tee) then cut to one SBG Co. piece that does all three, worn by a real woman in a real carpool line.

**Proof to surface:** 426 products across faith, mom humor, patriotic, and lifestyle humor — all targeted at the same woman, because she is not a niche, she is the whole thing.

**CTA:** See what we mean → | **Funnel:** cold

*Notes: Do not mention 'Christian apparel' or 'faith brand' in the first line. Lead with the identity fragmentation she feels but has never named. Let the reveal that this is a faith-forward brand land mid-scroll or in the visual, not in the hook. Amber is allergic to being sold religion — she wants to be seen as a whole person first.*

---

### Problem Aware

**Angle:** Her exact frustration — faith apparel that looks dated and preachy, or secular apparel with no faith layer — is the founding reason SBG Co. was built.

**What they know:** She has tried to find faith apparel that doesn't embarrass her and come up empty. She has typed 'cute Christian tees that don't look churchy' into Pinterest. She has settled for generic boutique tees that are fun but feel hollow. She knows the gap exists — she just hasn't found anyone who fills it.

**Gap to fill:** SBG Co. exists and was built specifically for this frustration. She also doesn't know that the kids pieces coordinate with hers — so the mom-and-mini photo she has been trying to style with mismatched pieces has a real solution.

**Hook style:** pain-callout / commiseration opener

**Example hook:** *"If one more Christian store tries to sell me a tee that looks like it came with a bookmark and a WWJD bracelet, I am going to lose it."*

**Framework:** `pas` | **Mechanic:** Commiseration-to-Reveal — talking head (founder or relatable mom) venting about the exact problem in Amber's own language, then pivoting to show the product as the obvious answer she has been looking for.

**Proof to surface:** 10 years building this brand because the founder couldn't find what she wanted either. Made to order in Charlotte, NC — not a fast fashion dropship operation.

**CTA:** This is what you have been looking for → | **Funnel:** cold

*Notes: Use her exact search language: 'cute Christian tees that don't look churchy.' This is the hook that earns the tap. The competitor indictment (Kerusso, Christian bookstore aesthetic) should be implied by the visual, not stated by name — show the dated aesthetic, don't sue it.*

---

### Solution Aware

**Angle:** Other modern faith brands serve the devotional side of who she is. SBG Co. is the only brand that also handles the feral mom, the Diet Coke obsession, and the Fourth of July cookout — because she is all of it, not just one thing.

**What they know:** She knows brands like Elevated Faith exist. She has seen faith apparel that is cleaner and more modern. She is actively comparing options and deciding which brand actually gets her full identity — not just the devotional side of it.

**Gap to fill:** SBG Co. is the only option in the category that serves all four of her message lanes (faith, mom humor, patriotic, lifestyle humor) in one place, with coordinating kids and baby pieces — so she can shop once and dress the whole family in pieces that actually go together.

**Hook style:** differentiation-frame / category-comparison

**Example hook:** *"Most faith apparel brands make tees for your Sunday self. We make tees for your Tuesday self too — the one who needs a Diet Coke and is raising her kids feral."*

**Framework:** `fab` | **Mechanic:** The Full Closet Walk — a flat-lay or carousel showing the range: a faith tee, the FERAL pullover, a Diet Coke tee, a kids onesie, and a mom-and-mini coordinating set. No competitor named. Just the breadth of who she is, all in one brand.

**Proof to surface:** 4,000+ boutique wholesale partners on Faire — the boutique buyers who curate for this exact woman have already voted. Featured on the Today Show.

**CTA:** Shop the full edit → | **Funnel:** cold

*Notes: This is the stage where SBG Co. wins on breadth. Elevated Faith is her comparison benchmark — SBG Co. beats it by being broader and warmer, not more minimal or devotional. Do not try to out-devotional Elevated Faith. Win on 'the whole you' positioning.*

### Product Aware

**Angle:** Every objection she has about quality, fit, and timing can be answered by the made-to-order process and the proof that 4,600+ women have already ordered and loved it.

**What they know:** She has seen SBG Co. on Instagram, in a friend's post, or on Faire. She knows the aesthetic is right. She is hesitating on: will it fit like a normal shirt, will it arrive in time, does it come in her size, and will she look try-hard or preachy wearing it.

**Gap to fill:** Made to order means it is made specifically for her — not a warehouse closeout. The made-in-Charlotte story is a quality signal, not a liability. The phrases are designed to feel warm and confident, not preachy — the Today Show featured it, meaning it passed the mainstream taste filter.

**Hook style:** objection-annihilator / social proof cascade

**Example hook:** *"Before you talk yourself out of it — it fits like a normal tee, ships from Charlotte, and 4,600 women already own one. Your only regret will be not ordering sooner."*

**Framework:** `pastor` | **Mechanic:** Review Parade with Fit Shot — UGC-style carousel or reel: real women wearing SBG Co. in real life contexts (school pickup, cookout, church lobby), interspersed with five-star review quotes addressing each objection (fit, quality, timing, message tone).

**Proof to surface:** 4,600+ Faire orders. Today Show feature. Made to order in North Carolina. Verified buyer reviews calling out fit and quality specifically.

**CTA:** Shop now — made just for you → | **Funnel:** warm

*Notes: The made-to-order model is her biggest latent objection (timing anxiety). Address it directly and frame it as a quality signal, not a warning. 'Made specifically for you' reframes wait time as craft, not inconvenience. Pull real review quotes if available — do not write fake review language.*

### Most Aware

**Angle:** She already knows she wants it. Give her permission to buy it now, remind her the mom-and-mini moment is more urgent than she thinks, and hand her the path to checkout.

**What they know:** She wants it. She is in the cart or close to it. She needs a final push — urgency, reassurance, or a reason to buy today instead of closing the tab.

**Gap to fill:** There may be a limited seasonal window, a holiday deadline for made-to-order lead time, or a specific bundle opportunity (grab the matching kids piece while she is here) she has not seen yet.

**Hook style:** urgency-close / aspiration-crystallizer

**Example hook:** *"You have been screenshot-saving this for a week. The matching onesie for your baby is right there. Order today and wear both by the weekend."*

**Framework:** `aida` | **Mechanic:** The Screenshot-to-Cart Close — retargeting ad showing the exact product she viewed, paired with the coordinating kids piece she did not see, with a countdown or seasonal urgency message. Static image or short video.

**Proof to surface:** Made to order — meaning once you order, this is made for you specifically. Not a mass-production item. If you want it for the holiday, the clock is real.

**CTA:** Complete your order → | **Funnel:** retargeting

*Notes: Do not over-discount at this stage — Amber is buying on identity and aesthetics, not price. A discount can cheapen the brand signal she is buying into. Urgency from seasonal lead time or mom-and-mini bundling is more on-brand than a promo code. If a limited edition or seasonal design is relevant, lead with that instead.*

## Grandma Gift-Giver Gloria *(secondary)*

### Unaware

**Angle:** The most meaningful baby gift is one the mom actually dresses the baby in — and this brand makes the ones she shows off, not the ones she stores in the drawer.

**What they know:** Gloria buys baby gifts from Target, Amazon, and Pottery Barn Kids. She thinks of baby gifts as practical — onesies, blankets, rattles. She wants to give something meaningful but has not conceptualized that apparel with a faith message can be giftable, meaningful, and actually something her daughter will use.

**Gap to fill:** There is a brand that makes faith-message baby and kids apparel beautiful enough that her daughter will put it on the baby, photograph it, and text Gloria a picture — and that the same brand makes pieces her daughter can wear herself so Gloria can gift the whole family moment.

**Hook style:** story-opener / emotional-future-pacing

**Example hook:** *"The baby gifts that get photographed and shared are never the ones from the registry. They are the ones someone chose because they actually knew the family."*

**Framework:** `bab` | **Mechanic:** The Photo Text Moment — a short video or carousel showing a grandma's phone screen: a text from her daughter with a photo of the baby in an SBG Co. onesie and a heart emoji. No words needed. The emotion lands without selling.

**Proof to surface:** Heaven Sent Onesie, Wild Like My Curls, and a full baby collection designed around faith and personality — not generic pastel patterns.

**CTA:** Find a gift she will actually love → | **Funnel:** cold

*Notes: Gloria is not searching for SBG Co. — she is being found. This cold ad should run wherever her daughter's demographic runs (Facebook, Instagram) with targeting that reaches women 55–72. The hook should feel like something Gloria would forward to her daughter — not like a store ad. Avoid product-first framing. Lead with the gift moment, not the product.*

### Problem Aware

She has looked at Christian bookstore baby items and felt the dated aesthetic was wrong for her modern daughter. She is stuck.

**Gap to fill:** SBG Co. exists as the bridge: faith-meaningful without being cheesy, baby apparel that a stylish young mom would actually dress the baby in, and giftable in a way that signals she really thought about it.

Heaven Sent onesie or faith-message baby tee. Clean, warm, beautiful. Let the aesthetic do the argument.

**Proof to surface:** Made to order in Charlotte, NC — not a mass-market product. Today Show feature as a trust signal she can tell her daughter about.

The Today Show mention is the credibility signal that works on Gloria — it is the mainstream proof she needs to feel safe ordering from a brand she has never heard of.*

**Angle:** Her customers are already wearing this brand — they are just buying it direct instead of through her boutique, and she is losing the sale.

**What they know:** Brooke buys for a customer who is a stylish Southern or Midwestern Christian mom. She carries faith-adjacent and lifestyle apparel from Faire. **Gap to fill:** There is a faith-forward apparel brand that her own customer is already buying direct — which means Brooke is leaving margin on the table. SBG Co.'s wholesale catalog is available on Faire and was built specifically for boutiques like hers.

**Hook style:** lost-revenue-pattern-interrupt / competitive-threat

Faire page with 4,600+ orders. Text: 'You should be the one selling this.'

**Proof to surface:** 4,600+ Faire orders. 4,000+ active boutique wholesale partners. The brand already has traction in her market segment — she is the gap.

**CTA:** See it on Faire → | **Funnel:** cold

*Notes: Brooke is a business buyer, not an emotional buyer. The hook needs to speak to her in revenue terms, not lifestyle terms. She does not need to be sold on the brand's mission — she needs to be sold on the revenue opportunity and the proof of demand. Lead with the Faire order count and the floor moment scenario.*

## Cross-stage observations

**Highest leverage stages:** solution_aware (Amber) — This is SBG Co.'s single biggest competitive battleground. Amber is actively comparing and SBG Co. wins decisively on breadth and tone. The 'all of you, not just your Sunday self' angle is unmatched in the category and should receive the largest creative investment., problem_aware (Gloria) — Gloria is highly reachable at this stage via her daughter's social graph and shower/holiday timing. The friction is not desire, it is confidence. A single well-executed trust-building ad here closes the gift purchase with minimal funnel complexity., product_aware (Brooke) — Brooke is a high-value converter who needs information, not inspiration. One strong buyer-brief-style creative at this stage likely converts a $500–2,000 first wholesale order. The ROI per converted buyer is significantly higher than DTC.

**Weakest stages:** unaware (Gloria) — Cold awareness for a 55–72 demographic requires Facebook placement and social proof from her daughter's world. Without VOC data or a daughter-referral mechanic, this stage is expensive to execute well and should be deprioritized unless SBG Co. has strong Facebook creative infrastructure., unaware (Brooke) — Reaching boutique buyers cold without Faire's native discovery algorithm or a trade show presence is difficult. The highest-leverage cold channel for Brooke is Faire's own trending/new section, not paid social. Paid cold ads for Brooke should be considered secondary to organic Faire optimization., most_aware (Amber) — Amber is emotionally ready to buy but price-sensitive to discounting. Urgency mechanics here are limited to seasonal lead time and mom-and-mini bundling — which are strong but require seasonal creative refreshes to stay relevant. A static evergreen retargeting ad will decay quickly for this persona.

**Budget/creative distribution:** For DTC (Amber-focused): allocate 40% of creative volume to solution_aware stage — this is the conversion engine. Allocate 30% to problem_aware cold traffic to fill the funnel. Allocate 20% to product_aware retargeting with UGC and review-led creative. Reserve 10% for most_aware cart-abandonment retargeting with mom-and-mini bundle close. For wholesale (Brooke-focused): invest the majority of touchpoints in Faire organic optimization and product_aware direct outreach rather than paid social. For gifting (Gloria-focused): time paid social to the 8-week window before peak gifting seasons (Christmas, Easter, Mother's Day) with problem_aware creative targeted at the 55–72 demographic on Facebook.

**Category notes:** SBG Co. operates in a category — faith apparel — where the dominant incumbent aesthetic (Kerusso, Christian bookstore) is so distinct and so dated that category indictment is a nearly frictionless creative angle. Ads that simply show the contrast between the old aesthetic and SBG Co.'s modern execution will outperform explanation-heavy creative at every stage. The made-to-order model is simultaneously the brand's most defensible quality proof and its most commonly cited purchase barrier — every creative touchpoint should proactively reframe wait time as craft signal, not operational limitation. The mom-and-mini coordinate structure is SBG Co.'s highest-leverage product mechanic and most underdeveloped creative angle — a disproportionate share of future creative should visualize the family moment (mom and child in coordinating SBG Co. pieces) because no competitor can replicate this and it is the highest emotional trigger for both Amber and Gloria.
