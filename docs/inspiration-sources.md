# Inspiration sources: where to look when the ideas run dry

Read this when the operator says he does not like any of the ads, is not sure what to do next, or wants something
more original than the usual e-commerce ad. Read it too when you cannot find a fresh direction for a brand.

It lists outside places to look and how to borrow from them. It is not a production route. The briefing gates,
the client's own look and every production rule still apply to whatever comes out of it.

## How to use a source

1. **Offer it, do not wander.** Name the source and say why: "we could look at Adfolio". Go on the operator's yes.
   Do not browse inspiration sites unasked on a normal job.
2. **Look, then name the move.** For each example worth keeping, write one line on what it does to stop the
   scroll. A move is an idea or a layout, such as "one visual joke with a small headline". It is not the artwork.
3. **Rebuild it for the client.** Use the client's product, facts, colours and type. Never copy an example's
   artwork, words, logo or masthead. Never imitate a real publication or another brand.
4. **Propose before building.** Show three to five one-line directions first (the `creative-variant-explorer`
   skill), then build what the operator picks.
5. **Keep the rules.** Copy stays true and simple. Paid generation needs a quote and a go. A style test is
   labelled as a test and is not launched. The client's `creative-preferences.md` wins over any borrowed style.
6. **Keep what worked.** An example worth keeping goes into the Ad Reference Library through `adc library save`
   (see `docs/ad-analyzer-openclaw.md`). Never hand-write a library card, and do not save copies of other
   people's ads or covers anywhere else in this repo.

## Source 1: Adfolio

https://www.adfolio.design/

A hand-picked gallery of B2B software ads from Meta and LinkedIn, each with a short note on why it works.

- **Why it helps.** Software has no product photo to lean on, so the idea has to carry the ad. That is the part
  a routine e-commerce ad often lacks. The operator named it as the place to go for out-of-the-box ads.
- **What is there** (seen in November 2026): statics on the home page, plus GIFs, videos, landing pages and
  themed collections.
- **Filters:** industry, platform, offer and angle. The angles are before/after, benefit first, capability first,
  case study, comparison, feature first, meme, pain point first, question and testimonial. Start from the angle
  that fits the brief, not from the industry.
- **How to borrow.** These are not shop ads. Take the idea or the layout move and ask what the product's version
  of it is. A "comparison" ad for software becomes the product against the thing it replaces.
- **Practical.** It can be browsed without signing in. Decline its cookie notice.

## Source 2: magazine covers

Treat the first frame of an ad as a magazine cover: a static, the first card of a carousel, a video's opening
frame. A cover has one job, which is to make someone stop.

- **Where to look:** Bloomberg Businessweek covers from about 2010 to 2014, Noble Rot, Buffalo Zine, Nautilus and
  Acne Paper. Any magazine with strong art direction works. Search for cover galleries and look; do not save the
  covers.
- **How to make the ads:** follow the `magazine-cover-ads` skill
  (`.claude/skills/magazine-cover-ads/SKILL.md`). It lists the cover moves and how to build each one from a
  client's own product photos.

## Adding a source

Add a section here by pull request: the link, one line on what it is, why it helps, and how to borrow from it.
Keep it short. A source earns its place when the operator has named it or it has produced an ad he kept.
